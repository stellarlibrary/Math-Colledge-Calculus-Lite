# 🏺 Surface Area of Revolution

*Find the outer area of a 3D surface by revolving tiny arc-length ribbons around an axis.*

## Core Formula

General formula (for rotation about any axis):

$$
S = \int 2\pi r \, ds
$$

- **Revolved about the $x$-axis** (radius $r = y = f(x)$ on $[a, b]$):

$$
S = \int_a^b 2\pi f(x)\sqrt{1 + \big[f'(x)\big]^2}\,dx
$$

- **Revolved about the $y$-axis** (radius $r = x$ on $[a, b]$):

$$
S = \int_a^b 2\pi x\sqrt{1 + \big[f'(x)\big]^2}\,dx
$$

## What It Means

When you spin a curve $y = f(x)$ around an axis, it sweeps out a hollow 3D shell (like a vase, trumpet, or lamp shade). To find its surface area, integrate the circumference **$2\pi r$** traveled by each point times the slant arc-length **$ds$** of the curve.

## Intuition

Take a tiny segment of the curve of slant length $ds$ at distance $r$ from the axis of rotation. Spinning that segment once around the axis traces a thin **frustum band (ribbon)** with:
- Circumference $= 2\pi r$
- Slant width $= ds = \sqrt{1 + (y')^2}\,dx$
- Band area $= 2\pi r\,ds$

Why use $ds$ instead of $dx$? Because a steep slope has much more surface area over the same horizontal width $dx$ than a flat slope!

## Example

**Problem:** Find the surface area generated by revolving $y = \sqrt{x}$ on $[1, 4]$ about the $x$-axis.

Here $r = y = \sqrt{x}$ and $y' = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, so $(y')^2 = \frac{1}{4x}$:

$$
S = \int_1^4 2\pi\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4x}}\,dx = 2\pi \int_1^4 \sqrt{x + \frac{1}{4}}\,dx
$$

$$
= 2\pi \left[\frac{2}{3}\left(x + \frac{1}{4}\right)^{3/2}\right]_1^4 = \frac{4\pi}{3}\left[\left(\frac{17}{4}\right)^{3/2} - \left(\frac{5}{4}\right)^{3/2}\right] = \frac{\pi}{6}\left(17\sqrt{17} - 5\sqrt{5}\right)
$$

## Common Mistakes

- **Using $dx$ instead of $ds = \sqrt{1 + (y')^2}\,dx$**: $\int 2\pi y\,dx$ misses the slant factor completely.
- **Picking the wrong radius $r$**: $r$ is always the distance **from the axis of rotation to the curve** ($r = y$ for the $x$-axis; $r = x$ for the $y$-axis)—independent of whether you integrate with respect to $x$ or $y$.

## Remember

> Surface Area $=$ **$\int (\text{circumference}) \times (\text{arc length}) = \int 2\pi r\sqrt{1 + (y')^2}\,dx$**.
